Morse code · Lesson 3 · about 13 minutes

All light

Today you add S, the first character with no heavy beat in it at all. It starts exactly like R, so the trick from Lesson 2 (hear how it starts) won't be enough on its own. You'll learn a second thing to listen for: whether a character has a bump in it.

Warm-up: recall before you hear

Hum K, M and R from memory first. Then run the drill. Do this on a different day from your last Lesson 2 session if you can: a score after a night's sleep tells you what has actually stuck.

If this comes out under 80%, go back to Lesson 2 for today. That's not a setback: it just means the last one needs one more night.

Meet S

S is di-di-dit. Play it a few times and say it along with the tone.

S

di-di-dit

At 20 WPM the whole of S lasts 300 ms, a little over half as long as K. It doesn't sound like three of anything. It sounds like one quick flutter. Hold on to that: don't count the dits. Later you'll meet H (four dits) and 5 (five), and nobody counts those at speed. You'll know them the way you know S, by how long the flutter runs. If you start counting now, those will be much harder.

S against R

R and S both open light, so the start won't tell them apart. Listen to the middle instead. R has a bump in it, a heavy beat that sticks up, and S stays flat and light the whole way through. Play these a few times.

S and M are opposites: one is all light and the other all heavy. You'll rarely mix those two up. K and R each have a mix of both. So you now have two questions to ask a sound, and they cover everything so far:

Quick check (no scrolling up)

R and S both start light. What tells them apart at full speed?

R has a heavy beat in the middle, and S is flat throughout. Counting is the trap this lesson warns about. Both end on a light dit, so the ending doesn't help. They're close in length too (S is 5 units, R is 7), which is too close to time by ear.

Play this and name it:

S: di-di-dit. Light all the way through, with no bump.

And this one:

R: di-dah-dit. Light start, then the bump.

Drill 1: hear it

K, M, R and S mixed together, with S coming up about half the time. Keys: k, m, r, s. When you miss, listen to the replay for the bump (or for the lack of one).

Goal: 90% over your last 20 answers. Expect R/S to be your new most-confused pair, now that K/R is settled. With four characters, a slightly slower median than Lesson 2 is normal. It should come back down within a session or two.

Drill 2: send it

Key each letter you see with the space bar. S looks easy, but it tests two things:

  • Keep the dits even. Three quick, equal flicks. If one runs long, the decoder hears a dah and you've sent R or U.
  • Keep the gaps tight. With no dah to fill the time, it's easy to pause between dits. A gap longer than about 2½ dits and a receiver may hear two letters, like I and E.

Your Lesson 2 sending was 11/12 at 15 WPM, but your dah : dit ratio was 2.4, a bit under the 3 you're aiming for. Either your dahs are a touch short or your dits a touch long, and the bars show which. Stay at 15 WPM for this lesson (the drill remembers its speed separately, so set it once) and work on bringing the ratio up: compare your bars with the ideal ones to see which end needs fixing, and make S's dits real flicks. When the ratio reads 2.7 or more and you're getting 11/12 or better, move up to 20.

In the "you vs ideal" bars after an S, the three bars should be the same width with small, even gaps. After a K or R, check whether your dahs or your dits are the ones off from the ideal.
