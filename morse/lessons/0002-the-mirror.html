Morse code · Lesson 2 · about 12 minutes

The mirror

Today you add R, your third character. It's built from the same pieces as K, in mirror image. By the end you'll pick K, M and R apart by ear at full speed and key all three.

Warm-up: recall before you hear

Before you press anything, hum K and M from memory, out loud or in your head. Then run this short drill. Recalling something you learned a day or two ago, before seeing it again, is what moves it into long-term memory.

If this comes out under 80%, spend today on Lesson 1 again and come back to this one tomorrow. Adding a character before the old ones are solid only slows you down.

Meet R

R is di-dah-dit. Play it a few times and say it along with the tone.

R

di-dah-dit

Now compare it with K. Both have three parts with a long beat among them, but the order is flipped: K is long-short-long and R is short-long-short. Play the pair a few times.

K then R

R then K

all three

K leans in on a heavy beat, and R trips in on a light one. This is the one thing you need to listen for: how a character starts. At 20 WPM the opening dit of R lasts 60 ms, far too short to count. But you can still hear that it started light. That is exactly what hearing Morse as shapes means.

Why "di" in the middle and "dit" at the end? Only a dot that ends a character gets its full "t", because that's where the sound stops. Inside a character it's clipped, just as it is when heard at speed.

Quick check (no scrolling up)

When you hear K and R at full speed, what is the quickest way to tell them apart?

Listen to the start. R opens light and K opens heavy. Counting is too slow at 20 WPM. Waiting for the ending doesn't help either, because both end in the same element as they began. Their total lengths are close too (R is 7 units, K is 9), so timing the whole character is unreliable.

Play this and name it:

R: di-dah-dit. It opened on the light beat.

And this one:

K: dah-di-dah. It opened on the heavy beat and ended on one too.

Drill 1: hear it

Now K, M and R are mixed together. R comes up about half the time because it's new. Keys: k, m, r. Answer on instinct, and when you miss, listen to the replay for the onset.

Goal: 90% over your last 20 answers. K/R mix-ups are the normal mistake at this stage. M is rarely the one you miss, so if you're mostly confusing K and R, you're making the expected errors and they'll fade.

Drill 2: send it

Key each letter you see with the space bar. R is the new challenge for your hand: the first dit has to be a quick tap, or it turns into a dah and you've sent something else. Start at the speed you finished Lesson 1 at. The drill remembers it separately for this lesson, so set it once.

Watch the "you vs ideal" bars after an R. If the first bar is as long as the middle one, your dit is too long. Think of it as a flick, not a press.
