The late bump

Today you add U, your fifth character. It starts like S and has a bump like R, so neither of your two cues settles it alone. You'll add a third: where the bump lands.

Meet U

U is di-di-dah. Play it a few times and say it along with the tone.

It opens with the same light flutter as S, then lands on a heavy beat. S trails off and U arrives somewhere.

U against R

This is the pair to really listen to. R and U are built from exactly the same pieces (two dits and one dah) and they're exactly the same length, 7 units. The only difference is where the bump sits: in the middle for R, at the end for U.

One way to hear it: R is a hop, up and back down, and U is a run-up followed by a jump. Same pieces, different shape. That's the whole idea of hearing Morse as shapes: you aren't adding up parts, you're hearing the outline.

Your three cues so far: how it starts (K from R), whether it has a bump (R from S), and now where the bump lands (R from U). Don't run through them as a checklist. They're names for what your ear is already doing.

Quick check (no scrolling up)

R and U share the same pieces and the same length. What tells them apart?

R's heavy beat is in the middle and U's is at the end. Both start light, both are 7 units long, and both have two dits, so nothing else separates them.

Play this and name it:

U: di-di-dah. A light run-up, then it lands heavy.

And this one:

R: di-dah-dit. The bump came in the middle, and it finished light.

Drill 1: hear it

All five characters mixed together, with U coming up about half the time. Keys: k, m, r, s, u. When you miss, listen to the replay for where the bump falls.

Goal: 90% over your last 20 answers. Expect R/U to be the pair you miss, and maybe S/U if you answer before U has finished. U only becomes U on its last beat, so let the sound end before you press.

Drill 2: send it

Your Lesson 3 sending was 12/12 at 15 WPM. Your dah : dit ratio rose to 2.5, but it's still under 2.7, so stay at 15 WPM one more lesson (set it once and the drill remembers it).

Your last screenshot also showed why the ratio is low, and it's not what I guessed in Lesson 3. In that R, your dah was almost exactly the ideal length. Your two dits were too long, about 1½ times the ideal. So forget about holding dahs longer. The fix is at the other end: make your dits shorter. Tap and lift straight away, as if the key were hot.

U is a good letter to practise this on: two quick flicks, then one full press. Feel the difference in your finger.

The stats line now shows your average dit and dah in units next to the ratio, so you can see which end is off without guessing. Aim for 1 and 3, and get your dit down to about 1.2 before you worry about anything else. After each send, the drill also tells you if that letter's dits ran long or its dahs fell short, and you can compare the "you" bars with the "ideal" bars below them. Also check the gap before the last element: in that R it was half again as long as it should be. Keep the elements of a letter close together.
