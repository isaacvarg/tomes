# Morse Code Resources

## Knowledge

- [Standard: ITU-R M.1677-1, *International Morse code* (2009)](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-M.1677-1-200910-I/)
  The official definition of the characters and the timing (dash = 3 dots; gaps of 1, 3 and 7 units). Use for: any question about what is "correct".
- [Book: *The Art & Skill of Radio-Telegraphy* by William G. Pierpont, N0HFF](https://www.qsl.net/ng3p/haminfo/pierpont/contents.htm) ([CWops PDF](https://cwops.org/old/pdf/art-skill.pdf))
  The classic text on learning code as sound. Use for: method and motivation, especially ch. 29 "The Koch Researches" and ch. 7 "Listening or 'Reading'". It argues against charts and mnemonics.
- [Article: ARRL, "Learning Morse Code"](https://www.arrl.org/learning-morse-code)
  The US national amateur radio association's beginner page. It recommends learning by sound (dits and dahs) and points to Koch and Farnsworth trainers. Use for: method claims and finding vetted tools.
- [Article: Wikipedia, "Morse code"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morse_code)
  A well-cited summary. Use for: the PARIS standard word (50 units) and the unit formula T = 1200 / WPM ms.

## Practice tools (outside this workspace)

- [LCWO.net: Learn CW Online](https://lcwo.net/)
  A free browser-based Koch course that tracks your progress. Use for: extra listening practice between lessons, once you have a few characters.
- [ARRL W1AW code practice files](http://www.arrl.org/code-practice-files)
  Recorded practice at 5–40 WPM. Use for: later, when the whole alphabet is in.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [Long Island CW Club](https://longislandcwclub.org/)
  A large club running weekly video classes from beginner level up, with sound-first teaching and a code-buddy program. Use for: live practice with other learners.
- [CWops CW Academy](https://cwops.org/cw-academy)
  Free small-group classes with experienced operators, linked from the ARRL page. Use for: structured coaching once the alphabet is in.
- [r/morse](https://www.reddit.com/r/morse/)
  A general Morse learner community. Use for: quick questions and progress checks. *Not yet vetted for quality.*
